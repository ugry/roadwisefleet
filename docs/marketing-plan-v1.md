# RoadwiseFleet — Marketing & GTM Plan v1

**Founder's plan:** "first make it free and available for everyone, especially small truckers who has only one truck."
**My verdict:** the right instinct, with one dangerous execution — *free* is correct; *everyone, everywhere, at once* is how free products die of noise. Below: the refined plan.

---

## 1. What's right about your plan

- **Free for solo drivers is exactly right.** They are the liquidity side — the marketplace dies without them. Research confirmed: drivers' willingness to pay is near zero, their data (beacons, parking, fuel) IS the value they contribute.
- **One-truck owners are the strategic segment.** They're the majority of EU carriers, the least served, and they compound (a solo driver who grows to 3 trucks becomes a Fleet customer).
- **Free-first builds the brand moat** — no incumbent offers the driver a genuinely free operating cockpit (Trans.eu charges €164/mo; telematics sell hardware).

## 2. What's dangerous about "for everyone, immediately"

| Risk | Why it kills you |
|---|---|
| **Empty-marketplace impression** | A driver who installs and sees zero loads in his corridor never returns — and tells other drivers. "Available to everyone" guarantees this happens in corridors you haven't seeded. |
| **Noise > quality** | Unverified users posting junk loads/fake offers destroy trust — the exact asset the category runs on (SafePay's 99.8% is Trans.eu's moat). |
| **Support load** | Free users generate support tickets. 5,000 free solo drivers with no revenue = a full-time support job for a solo founder. |
| **Monetization resentment later** | "Free forever" promises break. Drivers must stay free forever; companies must know up front they'll pay later. Say it in the signup. |
| **Burn rate of attention** | Marketing to "everyone" (all of Europe, all languages) spreads thin. One corridor owned beats ten corridors sprinkled. |

## 3. The refined plan: Free for drivers · corridor-first · seeded, then opened

### 3.1 The pricing promise (say it on day 1)
- **Drivers: free, forever.** No limits, no hidden fees — this is the brand promise.
- **Small companies: free during early access** (post up to N loads/month), paid later (per-truck SaaS, ~€10–25). State this openly at signup — honesty is the anti-churn.
- **Trust add-ons later** (verification package, guaranteed payment) — the known-good freemium ladder from the similar-apps research.

### 3.2 Corridor-first launch (the single most important change)
Pick **one corridor** — e.g., **Poland ↔ Germany** (Europe's largest international hauler lane) or the founder's home network — and win it completely before opening corridor #2.
- Seed **10–20 known small companies** posting real loads in that corridor (loads pull drivers — the scarce side).
- Then invite **the first 100 drivers** (referral + the parking community hooks).
- Only when the corridor shows density (offers per load ≥ 3, driver sees ≥ 5 relevant loads) does the "everyone" switch flip — per corridor, in the local language.

### 3.3 The growth loops (free spreads free)
1. **The parking loop:** driver marks a free parking spot → the next driver benefits → both stay. This is the Facebook-group dynamic from our forum research — the group IS the incumbent, and we out-compete it with structure + language + map. Seed it corridor-by-corridor with a founding-driver group.
2. **The beacon loop:** a driver's empty-return beacon wins them a load → they tell their owner-operator peers. The "empty today → paid tomorrow" story is the referral message.
3. **The WhatsApp loop:** load posting and tracking already flow through WhatsApp — every notification carries the product to the *other* party (shipper sees the tracking link → becomes a customer).
4. **Referral mechanics:** drivers invite drivers → both get priority-placement of their beacons for a week (costs nothing, feels like a reward).

### 3.4 Channels (in order of cost-effectiveness for solo truckers)
1. **Facebook trucker groups** (the proven channel — Miejscówki na pauzę etc.): participate genuinely, seed parking data, don't spam.
2. **Truck stops & border corridors** (TR/PL): QR codes + the parking board in local languages.
3. **Owner-operator associations** (local, regional): demos at meetings.
4. **App Store optimization** in TR/PL/DE/EN + WhatsApp share links.
5. Content (1 post/week): "we recovered €840 in detention for a 3-truck fleet" — proof, not promises.

### 3.5 Funnel & metrics (measure from day 1 — already instrumented in the spec)
```
Landing visit → driver install → beacon created → first load offer → first awarded load → weekly active → referral
```
North-star metrics: **offers-per-load per corridor** (marketplace density) and **weekly-active drivers** (the free-phase trigger). If offers-per-load < 1, stop all new marketing and fix the corridor's load supply — density before breadth.

### 3.6 The 30 / 60 / 90 sequence
- **Days 0–30:** landing + waitlist live (done) · seed 10–20 corridor companies with real loads · recruit 20–50 founding drivers via FB groups · parking board seeded manually with 50 verified spots.
- **Days 30–60:** open driver signup in the corridor (1 language + EN) · referral loop on · first 100 active drivers · public demo org on the landing page.
- **Days 60–90:** corridor #2 (adjacent lane, e.g., DE→CZ/AT or TR→EU if founder network is Turkish) · first "detention recovered" case study · decide corridor #3 by data, not opinion.

## 4. What this plan requires before it starts (the honest preconditions)
1. The Android app + web dashboard must exist (planning is done; build comes next).
2. The corridor choice must be made from *your* network — my default is PL↔DE, but your phone book wins.
3. Offsite backup + privacy pages (the two live gaps) — you cannot market a product that loses leads or has no privacy policy.

## 5. Bottom line
**Free = yes. Everyone = no.** Free forever for solo drivers (the liquidity), free-early-access for companies with the paid promise stated up front, launched **one corridor at a time** with seeded supply first. You don't win trucking by being everywhere; you win by owning one lane completely — then the next one.
